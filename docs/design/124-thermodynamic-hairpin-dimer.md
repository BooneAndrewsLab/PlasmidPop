# 124. Free-energy hairpin and dimer checks

Issues #339-#344, 2026-10-30. Design note 121 measured the length
heuristics for hairpins, self-dimers and pair dimers against Primer3 and
found 71-82 % agreement; the maintainer decided the disagreements are fixed
in 1.21.0 rather than tracked. This item replaces the heuristics with a
nearest-neighbour free-energy calculation.

Code: `src/core/primers/structure.ts` (the model),
`structure.test.ts`, `criteria.ts` (`hairpinDg`, `dimerDg`),
`primerDesign.ts` (reports, penalty, pair check), `checkPrimers.ts`
(clone-check pairs). Gate: `src/test/oracle/hairpinDimer.test.ts`.

## The model

`hairpinEnergy(seq)` and `dimerEnergy(a, b)` (`selfDimerEnergy(seq)` is
`dimerEnergy(seq, seq)`) give ΔG in kcal/mol at 37 C, 50 mM Na+, the
conditions of `meltingTemperature` and of the oracle, as the best single
helix: Watson-Crick stacks (the SantaLucia 1998 set already used for Tm,
plus a salt term per stack), bulges, interior loops with mismatch stacking,
hairpin loops with a terminal mismatch, terminal mismatches and dangling
ends at the helix ends, an A/T closing penalty, and duplex or hairpin
initiation. It is a dynamic programme over the cells that can pair, with
loops of up to four unpaired bases a side (six gave the same flags to within
0.3 %, at 1.5x the time). The answer is never positive: a structure that
costs more than it gives is not formed. This is what Primer3's `thal` does
for a duplex or hairpin search; wobble (G.T) pairs, tri- and tetraloop
bonuses and multi-branch structures are left out.

It is ours, not a port: no third-party code or tables are used. The stack
energies are the published set already in `thermo.ts`; the loop
initiations are SantaLucia and Hicks (2004) in round numbers; the mismatch
stacking, the end terms, the salt term, the duplex initiation and the loop
tables were then **fitted to Primer3's output** (coordinate descent on the
squared error of ΔG over the committed 300 primers and 199 pairs, and
smoothed by hand where the fit made a table jumpy). So agreement on that
set is partly by construction; the independent evidence is two further
sets, below.

## Result of the fix

Agreement with Primer3 on which primers are flagged (hairpin dG <= -2,
dimers dG <= -6 kcal/mol), on the committed set (seed 20261030, 300 primers,
199 pairs):

| Check      | Before |  After | Both | Ours only | Primer3 only | Neither |
| ---------- | -----: | -----: | ---: | --------: | -----------: | ------: |
| Hairpin    | 82.0 % | 94.7 % |   70 |         4 |           12 |     214 |
| Self-dimer | 79.7 % | 95.7 % |  101 |         4 |            9 |     186 |
| Pair dimer | 70.9 % | 95.5 % |   36 |         6 |            3 |     154 |

The 90 % target is met for all three. The six disagreement classes of note
121 shrink from 35/19/27/34/35/23 to 4/12/4/9/6/3 cases (#339-#344).

**ΔG error against Primer3** (ours clipped at 0, Primer3's positive values
counted as 0): hairpin mean 0.26 kcal/mol, max 2.26; self-dimer mean 0.70,
max 4.31; pair dimer mean 0.49, max 5.20. On the structures that matter
(Primer3 dG <= -2) the means are 0.49, 0.69 and 0.50. The largest errors are
long G/C-rich primers where Primer3 finds a more stable multi-region
dimer than a single helix with short loops; they are far past both cutoffs.
`structure.test.ts` gates the means and maxima and checks named primers
from the issues.

**Held out.** Two sets generated by the same script with other seeds (777,
4242; primer3-py 2.0.3, 300 primers and about 200 pairs each, not committed)
were not used in the fit: hairpin 94.7 % and 91.3 %, self-dimer 92.0 % and
90.7 %, pair dimer 97.5 % and 97.0 %. The fit generalises, with a loss of up
to five points on self-dimers.

## The 3' rule, kept on purpose

`maxThreePrime` (default 4) still refuses a primer whose 3' end pairs with
itself, and a pair whose 3' end pairs with the other, over more bases than
that. It is a separate check: a few bases at a 3' end are extended by the
polymerase whatever their ΔG, which is not what ΔG measures. Against
Primer3's dimer dG alone it therefore flags more, and the test keeps those
counts apart as "ours only":

| Rule                     | Agreement | Ours only | Primer3 only |
| ------------------------ | --------: | --------: | -----------: |
| Self-dimer, ΔG + 3' rule |    90.7 % |        22 |            6 |
| Pair dimer, ΔG + 3' rule |    79.4 % |        39 |            2 |

The pair figure is the price of the rule, not an error in the ΔG. It is
the same rule that made #343 (35 ours-only flags); it is now documented as
intended, and #344 (internal G/C cross-dimers the 3'-only rule missed) is
closed by the pair ΔG check, which refuses a pair at or below `dimerDg`
wherever the duplex is.

## Where it shows

- `PrimerReport` has `hairpinDg` and `selfDimerDg` (the stem and stretch
  lengths are gone); `PrimerPair`, `AnchoredPair` and the clone-check pair
  have `crossDimerDg`, kcal/mol, 0 or less.
- Criteria: `hairpinDg` -2 and `dimerDg` -6 (`DEFAULT_HAIRPIN_DG`,
  `DEFAULT_DIMER_DG`), replacing `maxHairpin` and `maxSelfComplementarity`.
  A stored preference with the old fields falls back to the defaults, as
  any unreadable field does.
- A candidate is refused when its hairpin or self-dimer is at or below the
  cutoff; a pair is refused when the duplex of its two primers is. The
  ranking penalty takes 0.5 per kcal/mol of hairpin and 0.25 per kcal/mol of
  self-dimer, where it took 0.5 per stem base over 2 and per stretch base
  over 3; nothing else in the ranking changed. A pair's own ΔG does not
  enter its penalty.
- Settings: **Hairpin** and **Dimer** take a ΔG in kcal/mol. Check a primer
  and the results detail show ΔG; the **No hairpin** chip means a ΔG above
  -1 kcal/mol.
- Warning texts: `Hairpin, ΔG -2.6 kcal/mol`, `Self-dimer, ΔG -6.2 kcal/mol`.
- Speed: the free-energy scans cost about 0.05 ms per primer. The designer
  now checks Tm, GC and base run first and scans only the windows that pass;
  designing over 200 bp each side on a 3 kb template takes about the same CPU
  time as before (0.7 s against 0.6 s on a loaded workstation).
- The PCR panel's own primer-dimer warning (`primerDimers`, 3' pairing over
  `maxThreePrime`) is unchanged.

## Primer pairs oracle (#337, item 122)

Re-exported and regenerated after the change (`run.sh primer-pairs`; the
script as committed removes its input and then crashes on the first
vitest step, so the export and the Python step were run by hand):

| Measure                                                     | Before    | After     |
| ----------------------------------------------------------- | --------- | --------- |
| Our pairs Primer3 accepts outright                          | 177 / 200 | 181 / 200 |
| Refusals, all high hairpin stability (#345)                 | 23        | 19        |
| Our top pair equals Primer3's top pair                      | 0 / 40    | 0 / 40    |
| Our top pair overlaps Primer3's (both primers)              | 7 / 40    | 7 / 40    |
| Any of our 5 overlaps any of Primer3's 5                    | 18 / 40   | 18 / 40   |
| Our top pair worse than all Primer3's top 5, by its penalty | 36 / 37   | 38 / 38   |

The refusals left are Primer3's hairpin criterion, the Tm of the structure
(at most 47 C), which is a different measure from the ΔG used here; they
stay with #345. Ranking (#346) did not move. The gate in `primerPairs.test.ts`
is tightened to 90 % accepted and 19 refusals.

## Specificity follows `pcr` (#351)

The new ranking surfaced a pair in the clone-check designer
(`TTTCAGTGCCGTTCTTCTCCAGAC` / `TAAATGCATCAGATGGGGCCAGAC` on the tutorial
pLig-Vector + Insert-EcoRI-BamHI) whose forward primer anneals with its 3'
15 bases (2 mismatches) at a second site and primes there, so `pcr()` drew a
second band. The designer's `requireSpecific` rule asked only whether the
whole primer matched elsewhere. `annealsElsewhere` (`primerDesign.ts`) now
adds `pcr`'s own rule, `findAnnealingSites`, to that check; sites are told
apart by their 3' end. Both the primer designer's shortlist and the
clone-check designer use it, and `checkPrimers.test.ts` is back to asserting
one band per pair. The warnings in the cloning-end designers (`notSpecific`)
still count whole-annealing-part sites only; they are advice, not selection.
