# Data licences

PlasmidPop's code is released under the [MIT License](LICENSE). Some of the
data bundled with it is not the project's own and keeps the terms it was
published under. This file lists that data, where it came from and on what
terms. The files are generated by `scripts/build-feature-db.mjs`, which says
exactly where each record was fetched from; rerun it to rebuild them.

## Detect features: the curated core

`src/core/annotate/data/core-parts.json` (spec:
`scripts/feature-db/core-parts.json`)

Common plasmid parts — origins, markers, promoters, terminators, tags,
operators, recombination sites, reporters, primer sites — each read from a
public nucleotide record of the NCBI (GenBank/RefSeq). Every part cites the
record (`accession`, as accession.version) and where in it the bases lie
(`location`, a GenBank location). NCBI places no restrictions on the use or
distribution of the GenBank data
([NCBI policies](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/home/about/policies/)); the
submitters of some records may claim rights in them, which the citation lets
anyone check. The selection, names and notes are the project's and are
released under the MIT License with the code.

## Detect features: fluorescent proteins from FPbase

`src/core/annotate/data/fpbase.json` (spec:
`scripts/feature-db/fpbase-proteins.json`)

The fluorescent proteins, their names and their protein sequences are taken
from [FPbase](https://www.fpbase.org), whose data is licensed under the
[Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 4.0 International
licence](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/) (CC BY-SA 4.0).
This file is therefore distributed under CC BY-SA 4.0, not under the MIT
License, and anything made from it must be shared under the same terms.

- Attribution: Lambert, T.J. (2019) FPbase: a community-editable fluorescent
  protein database. _Nature Methods_ 16, 277–278.
  [doi:10.1038/s41592-019-0352-8](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41592-019-0352-8).
  Each entry links to its FPbase page (`fpbase`) and, where FPbase gives
  one, the paper that described the protein (`doi`); credit for each protein
  belongs to those authors.
- Changes: for each protein, the DNA is the coding sequence of the NCBI
  record named by FPbase's GenBank protein accession (`/coded_by`), kept only
  where it translates exactly to FPbase's protein sequence. Proteins for
  which that fails are left out.

Neither Addgene's, SnapGene's nor pLannotate's feature databases were used.
